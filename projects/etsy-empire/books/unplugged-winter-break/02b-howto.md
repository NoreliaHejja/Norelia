# Chapter 16: Step-by-Step – 20 Family Favorites in Detail

Some activities are so good that they deserve more than one line. Here are twenty favorites from the list, with everything you need to make them work.

## 1. The Ultimate Blanket Fort
**You need:** chairs, a table, 3–4 blankets or sheets, clothespins or binder clips, cushions, fairy lights or a flashlight.
**How:** Push two chairs back to back with space between them, or use a table as the roof. Drape the biggest blanket over the top and clip it to the chair backs. Add smaller blankets as walls. Fill the inside with cushions. Let the children decide what the fort is: a castle, a spaceship, a bear cave.
**Make it last:** Leave the fort up for a few days. Read stories, eat snacks or watch a family film inside it.

## 2. Indoor Obstacle Course
**You need:** cushions, a broom, painter's tape, a laundry basket, soft toys.
**How:** Create stations: jump over the cushion "river," crawl under the broom held between two chairs, walk the tape line like a tightrope, throw three soft toys into the basket, then run back. Time each round.
**Make it last:** Let the children redesign the course and challenge you.

## 3. Salt Dough Ornaments
**You need:** 2 cups flour, 1 cup salt, about ¾ cup water, cookie cutters, a straw, paint.
**How:** Mix flour and salt, add water slowly and knead into a smooth dough. Roll it out, cut shapes, and use the straw to make a hole for hanging. Bake at a low temperature (around 120°C / 250°F) for 2–3 hours until hard, or air-dry for a couple of days. Paint when cool.
**Make it last:** Write the year on the back. Hang them every Christmas.

## 4. Homemade Play Dough
**You need:** 1 cup flour, ½ cup salt, 1 tablespoon oil, about ½ cup warm water, food coloring.
**How:** Mix the dry ingredients, add oil and colored water, and knead until soft. Add a little flour if sticky. Store in a sealed container.
**Play ideas:** bakery, pizza shop, monster making, letter shapes.

## 5. Ice Excavation
**You need:** a plastic container, water, small waterproof toys, salt, droppers or spoons, warm water.
**How:** Freeze toys in layers of water overnight. Pop the ice block onto a tray and let children "rescue" the toys using warm water and salt.
**Learning bonus:** Talk about why salt and warm water help ice melt.

## 6. Snow (or Rain) Day Science: Rainbow Walking Water
**You need:** 6 clear cups, paper towels, water, food coloring (red, yellow, blue).
**How:** Arrange cups in a circle. Fill every other cup with water and add a different color to each. Fold paper towel strips to connect each cup to the next. Watch over a few hours as the colors "walk" and mix in the empty cups.

## 7. Kids' Café
**You need:** paper, crayons, aprons, simple snacks.
**How:** Children design a menu with drawings and prices, set the table, take your "order" and serve snacks. Use play money or buttons to pay.
**Make it last:** Open the café every afternoon at 4 p.m. during the break.

## 8. Family Game Tournament
**You need:** 4–6 games (cards, board games, simple paper games), a chart on the fridge.
**How:** Play one game each day. Award points for winning and for good sportsmanship. Crown a champion at the end of the break with a homemade medal.

## 9. Treasure Hunt
**You need:** paper, a small prize (a sticker, a treat, a coupon for a special activity).
**How:** Write simple clues that lead from one spot to the next ("Where we keep the cold milk" for the fridge). For pre-readers, use drawings instead of words.
**Make it last:** Let older children write a hunt for younger siblings.

## 10. Cardboard Box City
**You need:** boxes of all sizes, tape, markers, scissors (adult help for cutting).
**How:** Turn boxes into houses, shops, a garage, a tunnel. Draw windows, doors and signs. Add toy cars and figures.

## 11. Puppet Show
**You need:** old socks or paper bags, buttons, yarn, glue, a table turned on its side or a cardboard stage.
**How:** Make puppets together, then write a simple story with a beginning, a problem and a happy ending. Perform it for the family, or for grandparents on a video call.

## 12. Nature Scavenger Hunt
**You need:** a list (written or drawn), a bag.
**How:** Find: a pinecone, something red, a smooth stone, three different leaves, a feather, something that smells nice, something tiny. Back home, arrange the finds into a picture or a display.

## 13. Bird Feeder Pinecones
**You need:** pinecones, string, peanut butter or seed butter, birdseed.
**How:** Tie string to each pinecone, spread with butter, roll in birdseed and hang outside a window. Keep a bird diary of who visits.

## 14. Story Chain
**You need:** nothing (or paper to write it down).
**How:** One person starts a story with one sentence. Each person adds the next sentence. Silly is good. Write down the best ones and illustrate them.

## 15. Kitchen Science: Volcano
**You need:** a cup or bottle, baking soda, vinegar, dish soap, food coloring, a tray.
**How:** Put 2 tablespoons of baking soda in the cup, add a drop of soap and coloring, then pour in vinegar and watch it erupt. Build a "volcano" around the cup with play dough for extra fun.

## 16. Kindness Challenge
**You need:** a list of small kind acts.
**How:** Every family member does one kind act a day: make someone's bed, draw a card for a neighbor, share a toy, help with dinner. Talk about it at dinner.

## 17. Family Talent Show
**You need:** a "stage" (a rug), a flashlight spotlight, a program written by the kids.
**How:** Everyone prepares one act: a joke, a song, a dance, a magic trick, a cartwheel. Make tickets and invite the cat.

## 18. Shadow Puppets
**You need:** a flashlight or lamp, a blank wall, your hands.
**How:** Make a dog, a bird, a rabbit. Cut animal shapes from cardboard and tape them to sticks for more characters. Turn off the lights and tell a story.

## 19. The Time Capsule
**You need:** a box or jar, paper, photos, small objects.
**How:** Each person writes or draws: my favorite food, my best friend, what I want to be, my height. Add a photo and a small treasure. Seal it and write "Open next winter" on the lid.

## 20. Best-of-Break Memory Book
**You need:** paper, stapler or ribbon, crayons, photos if you have them.
**How:** At the end of each day, each child draws or writes one memory. On the last day, bind the pages into a little book. Read it together on the first evening back at school.

# Chapter 17: A Sample 14-Day Winter Break Plan

Here is one example of how two weeks might look. Change everything to suit your family. The idea is a gentle structure, not a timetable.

**Day 1 (Monday) – Maker Day:** family meeting about the break plan; salt dough ornaments; screen window after lunch; story chain at bedtime.

**Day 2 – Kitchen Day:** pizza faces for lunch; homemade play dough; walk to look at holiday lights.

**Day 3 – Adventure Day:** library visit; nature scavenger hunt in the park; family game tournament begins.

**Day 4 – Game Day:** board game in the morning; treasure hunt; video call with grandparents.

**Day 5 – Fort & Film Night:** build the ultimate blanket fort; family movie inside the fort.

**Day 6 – Weekend:** slow morning; long walk or sledding; baking for a neighbor.

**Day 7 – Weekend:** visit relatives (bring the activity bag); quiet evening with audiobooks.

**Day 8 – Science Day:** ice excavation (freeze the night before); rainbow walking water; volcano.

**Day 9 – Café Day:** the kids' café opens; puppet making; puppet show in the evening.

**Day 10 – Adventure Day:** a new playground or a museum; bird feeder pinecones.

**Day 11 – Kindness Day:** kindness challenge; toy clear-out for charity; cards for neighbors.

**Day 12 – Show Day:** family talent show rehearsals and performance.

**Day 13 – Weekend:** outdoor day; cardboard box city in the afternoon.

**Day 14 – Weekend:** time capsule; bind the best-of-break memory book; family review and plans for school weeks.

# Chapter 18: For You, the Parent

There's one more person whose relationship with screens matters during the break: you. Children learn far more from what we do than from what we say. If our phones are always in our hands, our rules about their screens will feel unfair, and they'll be right.

This isn't about guilt. Most parents use their phones for good reasons: work, family logistics, staying in touch, a moment of rest. But it helps to notice your own patterns, too.

## Small changes that make a big difference

- **Phone-free meals** for everyone.
- **A "phone parking spot"** in the kitchen, so your phone isn't in your pocket all day.
- **Say out loud what you're doing:** "I'm checking the weather for our walk," so children see purposeful use, not endless scrolling.
- **Protect your own rest.** If you need ten minutes of quiet, take it. A rested parent handles the hard moments better.
- **Be kind to yourself on the hard days.** Some days will go off-plan. That's normal. Tomorrow is a fresh start.
