# Chapter 14: 100 Screen-Free Games and Activities

Here they are: one hundred ideas to fill a winter break with play. Each one is marked with a suggested age range and how much setup it needs. Most need nothing more than what you already have at home.

**Key:** setup time (quick / medium / project) · Ages are just a guide; adapt freely.

![](ch2.jpg)

## How to use this list

Don't try to do everything. A hundred ideas is a menu, not a to-do list. Here are a few ways families use it:

- **Pick five favorites** at the start of the break and write them on the fridge. When someone says "I'm bored," point to the list.
- **Let children choose.** Read the list together and let each child circle the activities they want to try. Children engage far more with plans they helped make.
- **Match the activity to the energy.** Wild energy after lunch? Choose from the active games. Tired and grumpy at 5 p.m.? Choose something from the quiet list.
- **Prepare the night before.** Many activities work best when materials are ready to go: dough made, boxes collected, paper out on the table.
- **Repeat the hits.** If something works, do it again. Children love repetition, and a favorite game played ten times is ten good afternoons.
- **Mix in "nothing" time.** Not every hour needs an activity. Free, unstructured time is where some of the best play happens.

Most activities need only things you already have at home. Where something needs a little preparation, you'll find step-by-step instructions in the chapter that follows the list.


## Active Indoor Games (for wild energy and rainy days)

1. **Freeze dance.** Play music; when it stops, everyone freezes. *Ages 2+ · quick*
2. **Indoor obstacle course.** Cushions to jump, chairs to crawl under, a tape line to balance on. *3+ · medium*
3. **Balloon keepy-uppy.** Don't let the balloon touch the floor. Add rules: only elbows, only heads. *2+ · quick*
4. **Animal walks race.** Bear crawl, crab walk, frog jumps, penguin waddle across the room. *3+ · quick*
5. **Sock basketball.** Rolled-up socks into a laundry basket. Move it farther each round. *3+ · quick*
6. **Hallway bowling.** Plastic bottles as pins, a soft ball to roll. *3+ · quick*
7. **Simon says.** The classic. Let the kids be Simon too. *3+ · quick*
8. **Tape hopscotch.** Masking tape on the floor; hop through numbers or letters. *4+ · quick*
9. **Laser maze.** Criss-cross string or crepe paper in a hallway; get through without touching. *5+ · medium*
10. **Pillow fort war.** Two forts, soft "snowballs" of rolled socks. *5+ · medium*
11. **Kids' yoga story.** Act out a story with yoga poses: tree, mountain, cat, cobra. *3+ · quick*
12. **Musical statues, musical cushions.** Classic party games that never get old. *3+ · quick*

## Outdoor Winter Fun

13. **Snowman family.** Build one snow person for each member of the family. *3+ · quick*
14. **Snow painting.** Spray bottles filled with water and food coloring. *3+ · quick*
15. **Snow angels and footprint art.** Make patterns, letters or giant pictures with footprints. *2+ · quick*
16. **Ice suncatchers.** Freeze water with leaves, berries and twigs in a pie dish with a string loop. Hang outside. *3+ · project (overnight)*
17. **Nature scavenger hunt.** A pinecone, something red, a feather, three different leaves, an interesting stone. *3+ · quick*
18. **Bird feeder making.** Pinecones with peanut butter (or a seed-butter alternative) and birdseed. *3+ · medium*
19. **Winter walk bingo.** Make a bingo card: a dog, a chimney with smoke, a red door, a bird, a snowflake. *4+ · quick*
20. **Puddle (or snow) jumping contest.** Wellies essential. *2+ · quick*
21. **Sledding or cardboard sliding.** Any small slope will do. *3+ · quick*
22. **Track detectives.** Look for animal footprints in snow or mud. Guess who made them. *4+ · quick*
23. **Night walk with flashlights.** Look at the stars, holiday lights or the moon. *3+ · quick*
24. **Backyard camping hour.** Blankets, a thermos of cocoa and a flashlight. *4+ · medium*

## Kitchen Activities (cooking counts as learning!)

25. **Decorate cookies.** Plain cookies, icing, sprinkles. Maximum mess, maximum joy. *2+ · medium*
26. **Make pizza faces.** Ready dough or flatbreads; toppings become eyes, noses and hair. *3+ · medium*
27. **Homemade playdough.** Flour, salt, water, oil and food coloring. *2+ · quick*
28. **Salt dough ornaments.** Cut shapes, bake, paint. Hang on the tree. *3+ · project*
29. **Fruit kebabs.** Threading practice and a healthy snack. *3+ · quick*
30. **Hot chocolate bar.** Set up toppings; everyone designs their own. *3+ · quick*
31. **Kids' café.** Children make a menu, take orders and serve snacks to the family. *5+ · medium*
32. **Bake bread together.** Kneading is a wonderful sensory activity. *5+ · project*
33. **Homemade lemonade or smoothies.** Measuring, pouring and taste testing. *4+ · quick*
34. **Popcorn experiments.** Sweet, salty, cinnamon, cheese: vote for the best. *4+ · quick*

37. **Handmade cards** for grandparents, neighbors, teachers. *3+ · quick*

50. **Restaurant.** Menus, aprons, a play kitchen or real snacks. *3+ · medium*
51. **Post office.** Write letters, decorate envelopes, deliver to every room. *4+ · medium*
52. **Doctor or vet clinic** for stuffed animals. *3+ · quick*
53. **Space mission.** A cardboard rocket, a countdown and an alien planet in the living room. *4+ · medium*

72. **Library visit.** Let each child choose a stack of books. *2+ · quick*
73. **Reading picnic.** Blanket on the floor, snacks and books. *2+ · quick*
74. **Coloring and mandala pages.** Calming for kids and adults. *3+ · quick*
75. **Sticker scenes.** Create a whole story with stickers. *3+ · quick*

# Chapter 15: Making It Last Beyond the Break

When school starts again, it's tempting to slip back into old patterns. A few things will help you keep what worked:

- **Keep one or two "screen-free" anchors.** For example, no screens at dinner, or screen-free Sunday mornings.
- **Keep the invitation stations.** A basket of books and a box of art supplies in sight keep play going all year.
- **Hold a family review.** Ask: "What did we love about this break? What should we keep?"
- **Notice the wins.** Better sleep? Fewer meltdowns? More laughter? Name them out loud.

## A final word for parents

You don't have to be perfect to make a difference. Every walk, every board game, every afternoon in a blanket fort is a gift to your children: one they may not thank you for now, but that they will carry with them. And when the break is over and the house finally falls quiet, give yourself credit. You protected your family's time and attention in a world that constantly competes for it. That's no small thing.
