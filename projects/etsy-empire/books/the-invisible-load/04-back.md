# Your Fridge Card

Cut this out or copy it onto a card, and stick it where you'll see it.

[[box]]
**The invisible load, in one page**

- **Empty your head.** If it's in your head, write it down.
- **Make it visible.** Keep a family inventory with names next to each job.
- **Share whole jobs,** from noticing to finishing, not tasks.
- **Don't remind. Don't take it back.**
- **Look ahead together** for 15 minutes every week.
- **Kids are on the team.** One new job at a time.
- **Good enough is the standard.**
- **Rest is not a reward.** Protected time, every week.
- **Ask for help.** Specific, small, often.
[[/box]]

# The 30-Day Reset

Do one small step a day. If you miss a day, just carry on with the next one. If a step doesn't fit your family, swap it for something from the chapter it points to.

**Week 1: See it**

- **Day 1.** Brain dump: fifteen minutes, everything on paper (Chapter 1).
- **Day 2.** Mark every item D, S or K. Drop three D's today.
- **Day 3.** Hand over one small job completely (Chapter 1).
- **Day 4.** Read Chapter 2. Write out the hidden steps behind three tasks.
- **Day 5.** Rate your energy from 1 to 10. Check the burnout signs (Chapter 3).
- **Day 6.** Do one thing that finishes the stress cycle: a walk, a dance, a long hug.
- **Day 7.** Start your family inventory: three areas (Chapter 4).

**Week 2: Share it**

- **Day 8.** Finish the inventory for your five heaviest areas. Star what drains you.
- **Day 9.** Ask for a time to talk. Write your opening sentences on a card (Chapter 5).
- **Day 10.** Have the conversation. Agree on three whole jobs to hand over.
- **Day 11.** Write handover cards together (Chapter 6).
- **Day 12.** Don't remind. Notice the urge, and let it pass.
- **Day 13.** When asked "what should I do?", answer with a question (Chapter 7).
- **Day 14.** First weekly look-ahead (Chapter 9). Put it on the calendar as a repeating event.

**Week 3: Simplify it**

- **Day 15.** Set up one shared calendar, or tidy the one you have.
- **Day 16.** Write your family's top 20 meals.
- **Day 17.** Choose theme nights or a two-week meal rotation.
- **Day 18.** Create a standing grocery list.
- **Day 19.** Give each child one new job (Chapter 8). Show it once.
- **Day 20.** Set up a command center: calendar, forms tray, bag hooks.
- **Day 21.** Automate one bill or one regular order.

**Week 4: Protect yourself**

- **Day 22.** Choose one thing to do "good enough" this week (Chapter 10).
- **Day 23.** Agree on protected time for each adult (Chapter 11).
- **Day 24.** Take your first protected block. Don't do chores in it.
- **Day 25.** Write your *Things I used to love* list. Put one on the calendar.
- **Day 26.** One small daily rest: tea at the window, a short walk, headphones.
- **Day 27.** Ask someone for one specific swap or favor (Chapter 12).
- **Day 28.** Check in on the three handed-over jobs. Adjust the cards if needed.
- **Day 29.** Hand over one more whole job.
- **Day 30.** Do the brain dump again. Rate your energy again. Compare with Day 5.

Many women find that their list is shorter, their head is quieter and their energy number has moved, even if only by a point or two. That's real progress. Keep the weekly look-ahead and the protected time going, and repeat the reset whenever life gets heavy again (a new baby, a move, a new job, the holidays).

# Questions Women Often Ask

**What if my partner says the load is fine as it is?**
Show the inventory with the hidden steps. If they still disagree, suggest a one-month trial: three handed-over jobs, then a check-in. Results tend to be more convincing than arguments.

**What if we both work full time and there's simply too much?**
Then the answer is less, not just sharing. Drop what you can, simplify meals and activities, and consider paid help for one area if your budget allows (cleaning every two weeks, a grocery delivery, after-school care one day a week).

**I work part time or stay at home. Isn't the house my job?**
Some of it may be, by agreement. But the noticing and planning for the whole family, every hour of the day, including evenings and weekends, is more than any one job. Agree on what's yours during your working hours, and share the rest.

**My partner does things, but only when I ask. Is that progress?**
It's a start. The next step is ownership: whole jobs, from noticing to finishing, without your reminders (Chapters 6 and 7).

**What about same-sex couples, or families where the father carries the load?**
The load lands on whoever notices most, and that can be anyone. Everything in this book works the same way. Use whichever words fit your family.

**How long until things feel different?**
Many women feel lighter within the first week, just from getting the list out of their heads. Real change in who carries what usually takes a few months of check-ins and adjustments.

**What if I'm the one who struggles to let go?**
That's common, and you're not alone. Start with jobs that don't matter much to you, and use the three questions in Chapter 10.
