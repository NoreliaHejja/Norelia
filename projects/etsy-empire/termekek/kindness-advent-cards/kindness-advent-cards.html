How the kindness calendar works

A countdown that grows kind hearts instead of a sugar rush. Each card is one small mission that takes 5–20 minutes and needs little or no shopping.

1
Print and cut

Print the card pages on card stock, cut along the dashed lines and fold or roll each card.

2
Choose your calendar

Put a card in each pocket of an advent calendar, in numbered envelopes, in a jar, or let your Christmas elf deliver one every morning.

3
Do it together

Read the mission at breakfast and do it as a family. Little ones need a grown-up partner, older kids can lead.

4
Track and talk

Color a circle on the tracker every day. At dinner, pull a talk card and share how it felt.

Swap freely

Busy day? Swap cards around. There is a blank card page for your own family missions.

Grown-ups

Some missions need an adult (baking, litter walk, phone calls). Safety first, kindness second.

Kindness missions 1–6

1
Make a card for someone far away

Draw or write a card for a grandparent, cousin or friend who lives far away and mail it together.

Tip: add a family photo.
2
Hold the door

Hold the door open for someone today and say a friendly hello.

Tiny acts count too.
3
Thank a helper

Draw a thank-you picture for your teacher, bus driver, school nurse or lunch staff.

Name one thing they do for you.
4
Donate a toy

Choose one toy in good condition to give to a child who needs it this Christmas.

Grown-ups: find a local toy drive.
5
Compliment day

Give three different people a real compliment today.

"I like how you…" works great.
6
Help with dinner

Set the table, wash vegetables or stir the pot without being asked.

Ask: "What can I do?"

Kindness missions 7–12

7
Feed the birds

Hang a simple bird feeder or scatter seeds outside for winter birds.

A pine cone + seeds is enough.
8
Sweet note in a lunchbox

Hide a kind note in someone's lunchbox, coat pocket or bag.

Use the mini notes page.
9
Clean up together

Spend 10 minutes tidying a room you share, without complaining.

Put on a Christmas song and race the clock.
10
Bake for a neighbor

Bake or decorate cookies and bring a few to a neighbor.

Store-bought cookies count!
11
Call a grandparent

Make a phone or video call to a grandparent or older relative and ask about their childhood Christmases.

Ask: "What was your favorite gift?"
12
Share with a sibling

Let a brother, sister or friend choose first, or share a favorite thing.

Notice how they smile.

Kindness missions 13–18

13
Pick up litter

With a grown-up and gloves, pick up litter on a short walk.

Count how many pieces you find.
14
Make someone laugh

Tell a joke, make a funny face or plan a silly surprise.

Use a joke card from the back.
15
Thank you for the mail

Leave a thank-you note or drawing for your mail carrier or delivery driver.

Tape it to the mailbox.
16
Kindness for animals

Make a blanket bed for your pet, or collect old towels for an animal shelter.

Shelters often need towels.
17
Help without being asked

Find one job at home that needs doing and just do it.

Then see who notices!
18
Write a kind review of your day

At bedtime, tell your family one kind thing someone did for you today.

Say thank you to them, too.

Kindness missions 19–24

19
Snowflake surprise

Cut paper snowflakes and leave them for someone to find.

Write a kind word in the middle.
20
Teacher or coach gift

Make a small gift or card for a teacher or coach before the break.

A drawing is a perfect gift.
21
Be a good friend

Ask someone who is alone to play with you or sit with you.

Everyone wants to be included.
22
Kindness to yourself

Do something kind for you: rest, read, draw, or say "I did my best today".

You deserve kindness too.
23
Christmas Eve helper

Help get the house ready: wrap a gift, set out cookies or light the candles with a grown-up.

Ask what would help most.
24
Merry Christmas kindness

Give every person in your family a hug or a kind word today.

Look back at all 24 missions!
