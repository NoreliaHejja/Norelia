How to run your kindness calendar

A December routine that takes five minutes in the morning meeting and fills the classroom with good feelings, not more sugar.

1
Hang the wall calendar

Print the poster pages and display them by the door. Students color a square each day.

2
Reveal one mission a day

Read the mission card at morning meeting. Most missions take 5–20 minutes and need no shopping.

3
Track and celebrate

Use the class tracker to tick each finished mission. On the last day, hand out the Kindness Champion certificates.

4
Bring families in

Send the family letter home on day one so parents can talk about the missions at dinner.

Swap freely

School days, not calendar days: skip weekends and swap missions to fit your schedule.

Check first

For deliveries outside school (seniors, hospitals, shelters) check guidelines and permissions first.

Missions 1–6

1
Kind words wall

Each student writes one kind sentence about a classmate on a sticky note for the wall.

Teacher tip: Pair names from a hat so nobody is left out.
2
Thank the custodian

Make a big class thank-you card for the school custodian or cleaning staff.

Teacher tip: Deliver it together.
3
Compliment circle

Sit in a circle; everyone gives the person on their left a real compliment.

Teacher tip: Model one first.
4
Hold the door day

Hold the door for anyone coming behind you all day.

Teacher tip: Count the doors at the end of the day.
5
Reading buddies

Read a picture book to a younger class or a classmate.

Teacher tip: Older grades love this one.
6
Tidy-up team

Leave the classroom cleaner than you found it in 5 minutes.

Teacher tip: Play a song and race the clock.

Missions 7–12

7
Thank-you for the lunch staff

Draw pictures or write notes for the lunch team.

Teacher tip: Tape them near the lunch line.
8
Include someone

At recess, invite someone new to play.

Teacher tip: Talk about how it felt afterwards.
9
Kindness rocks

Paint or draw on small stones with kind words to hide around school.

Teacher tip: Paper stones work too.
10
Help a classmate

Offer help with something you are good at: reading, math, drawing, tying shoes.

Teacher tip: Make a 'we can help' list.
11
Letters to seniors

Write holiday cards for a local senior home.

Teacher tip: Ask families to help deliver.
12
Mystery kindness

Do one secret kind act today and don't tell anyone.

Teacher tip: Share how it felt, not what it was.

Missions 13–18

13
Teacher appreciation

Make thank-you cards for another teacher or the principal.

Teacher tip: Every class has a favorite.
14
Gratitude chain

Each student adds a paper link with something they're thankful for.

Teacher tip: Hang it across the room.
15
Animal kindness

Collect old towels or blankets for an animal shelter.

Teacher tip: Send a note home the week before.
16
Kind voice day

Use calm, kind voices all day. Notice when others do too.

Teacher tip: Give 'kind voice' shout-outs.
17
Welcome sign

Make a welcome sign for the school entrance.

Teacher tip: Ask the office first.
18
Thank the bus drivers

Write notes or draw pictures for the bus drivers.

Teacher tip: Hand them out at dismissal.

Missions 19–24

19
Pick up litter

Spend 10 minutes picking up litter on the playground (with gloves).

Teacher tip: Count and graph it for math.
20
Kindness bookmarks

Make bookmarks with kind messages for the school library.

Teacher tip: Ask the librarian to tuck them in books.
21
Partner praise

Tell your partner one thing they did well this week.

Teacher tip: Use 'I noticed…' sentences.
22
Cards for hospitals

Make cheerful cards for a children's hospital.

Teacher tip: Check the hospital's card guidelines.
23
Kindness pledge

Write one way you will keep being kind over the break.

Teacher tip: Display the pledges on the door.
24
Kindness party

Celebrate 24 days of kindness with a class party and certificates.

Teacher tip: Hand out the Kindness Champion awards.

Letter for families

Dear families,

This December our class is counting down to the winter break with a Kindness Calendar. Every school day we will complete one small kindness mission together: thanking school helpers, including classmates, making cards for our community and more.

You can help at home by asking your child, "What was today's kindness mission?" and by talking about the kind things you notice in your family.

A few missions may ask for donated items such as old towels for an animal shelter. We will always send a note home first, and joining in is completely optional.

Thank you for helping us grow kind hearts this season!

Warmly,
______________________ (teacher)
